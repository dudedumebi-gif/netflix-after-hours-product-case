# Research Analysis Framework

## 1. Analysis principles

1. Use the qualified sample as the denominator unless a question explicitly concerns affected users.
2. Report counts and percentages together.
3. Separate observed behaviour, recalled behaviour, stated preference, and prototype behaviour.
4. Report uncertainty and subgroup sample sizes.
5. Preserve disconfirming evidence.
6. Do not convert correlation, stated intent, or usability performance into revenue claims.

## 2. Quantitative preparation

### Exclusion rules

Exclude responses that meet predeclared criteria:

- Failed eligibility
- Duplicate response
- Failed attention check combined with another quality signal
- Completion time below a defensible minimum
- Nonsensical or copied open text
- Material internal contradiction indicating careless response

Keep an exclusion log with reason codes. Do not remove responses because they oppose the concept.

### Primary measures

| Measure | Denominator | Calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly sleep incidence | Qualified respondents | Reports two or more events per month / qualified respondents |
| Continuity impact | Affected respondents | Reports progression, lost position, or search / affected respondents |
| Moderate-high frustration | Affected respondents | Moderate through extreme / affected respondents |
| Core desirability | Affected respondents | Probably or definitely use / affected respondents |
| Estimated unintended minutes | Affected respondents | Midpoint estimate with open-ended top category sensitivity |
| Wearable permission comfort | Wearable owners and non-owners | Comfortable or very comfortable by segment |

### Statistical treatment

- Report 95% confidence intervals for major proportions.
- Use chi-square or Fisher's exact test for categorical subgroup differences when assumptions are met.
- Use non-parametric comparisons for ordinal measures where appropriate.
- Report effect sizes, not only p-values.
- Treat multiple subgroup tests as exploratory unless pre-specified.
- Do not weight the sample unless a defensible population frame and weighting plan exist.

## 3. Qualitative analysis

### Coding structure

- Viewing context
- Trigger and sleep journey
- Consequence
- Emotional response
- Workaround
- Control preference
- Recovery expectation
- Household conflict
- Privacy condition
- Accessibility concern
- Value signal
- Disconfirming case

Two-pass coding:

1. Deductive coding against the research questions
2. Inductive coding for unanticipated patterns

Create a participant-by-theme matrix and link each insight to anonymized evidence. Frequency language such as “most” or “few” must match the coded count.

## 4. Usability analysis

For every critical task, report:

- Unassisted success
- Assisted success
- Failure
- Median completion time
- Error types
- Confidence
- Issue severity

Round 1 identifies issues. Round 2 tests whether the revisions resolved them. Do not pool both rounds when claiming final task success.

## 5. Triangulation

| Evidence pattern | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Survey, interview, and usability agree | Strong directional evidence |
| Survey intent high but usability poor | Desirable concept with execution risk |
| Interviews strong but survey incidence low | Valuable niche; narrow segment or reposition |
| Problem frequent but frustration low | Weak urgency; reduce investment or simplify |
| Stated upgrade intent high without observed behaviour | Monetization hypothesis remains unvalidated |

## 6. Gate 2 scorecard

| Dimension | Pass | Conditional | Stop or reframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Meets approved threshold | Near threshold with concentrated segment | Materially below threshold |
| Severity | Meaningful frustration or consequence | Mild pain with strong niche | Little meaningful impact |
| Desirability | Core bundle meets threshold | Interest depends on redesign | Weak adoption intent |
| Usability | Critical tasks meet Round 2 threshold | Correctable high issues remain | Critical loss-of-control issue |
| Economic signal | Conservative pathway plausible | Requires uncertain assumptions | No credible pathway |

Gate 2 requires a written decision with supporting evidence, unresolved risks, and the disposition of every critical hypothesis.
