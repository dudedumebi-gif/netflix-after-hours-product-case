# Qualitative Interview Guide

## Session design

- Duration: 45-60 minutes
- Format: Semi-structured remote interview
- Participants: 12-15 across approved segments
- Recording: Only with explicit consent
- Objective: Understand real behaviour, language, consequences, alternatives, trust, and value before promoting the proposed solution

## Moderator opening

Thank you for participating. This is independent product research and is not affiliated with Netflix. We are exploring late-night streaming habits, including experiences that may be inconvenient or uneventful. There are no correct answers. I am testing the product idea, not you. Please avoid sharing account credentials, medical information, or anything confidential.

Confirm consent, recording choice, confidentiality limits, and the participant's right to stop.

## 1. Context and routine - 8 minutes

1. Tell me about when and where you usually stream video late in the day.
2. Which device do you normally use, and why?
3. Who else is usually present?
4. What role does streaming play in your bedtime or wind-down routine?
5. How does this change on weekends, shifts, travel days, or stressful days?

Probe for behaviour and context without assuming that falling asleep is a problem.

## 2. Most recent experience - 12 minutes

1. Walk me through the last time you became very drowsy or fell asleep while something was playing.
2. What were you watching?
3. What do you remember doing immediately beforehand?
4. What happened to the playback afterward?
5. How did you discover what had happened?
6. Did you try to find your place? Show me or describe how.
7. What, if anything, was frustrating?
8. Was anyone else affected?

Ask for concrete sequence, actions, and consequences. Avoid asking participants to estimate bandwidth or technical costs.

## 3. Current workarounds - 8 minutes

1. What do you currently do to prevent unwanted playback?
2. Have you used a TV timer, app timer, autoplay setting, phone automation, or end-of-episode control?
3. What works well?
4. Where does the workaround fail?
5. Why might you choose not to configure a timer?

## 4. Concept reaction - 12 minutes

Present the neutral concept description or prototype only after the behavioural discussion.

1. What do you think this feature is trying to do?
2. Which part feels most useful? Why?
3. Which part feels unnecessary or risky?
4. When would you use it?
5. When would you avoid it?
6. What would make you trust the saved recovery point?
7. What should happen when another person is still watching?
8. What language would make the feature easy to understand?

## 5. Automation and privacy - 8 minutes

1. Would you prefer a timer, schedule, inactivity signal, wearable signal, or combination?
2. What would you need to know before granting sleep-data access?
3. Does keeping raw health measurements on the device change your comfort level?
4. What would make wearable automation feel intrusive?
5. How should the system behave when permission is denied or data is unavailable?

Do not imply that the wearable can reliably determine the exact moment a person falls asleep.

## 6. Value and economic signal - 5 minutes

1. Would this affect how satisfied you feel with the service?
2. Could it influence whether you keep using the service? Why or why not?
3. Which feature, if any, could justify a higher-priced plan when combined with existing Premium benefits?
4. What would make the feature feel like a paywall rather than useful differentiation?

## 7. Close - 3 minutes

1. If you could change one thing about late-night streaming, what would it be?
2. What did I fail to ask?
3. May we contact you for a prototype follow-up?

## Moderator quality checklist

- Ask for the last real event before hypothetical opinions.
- Use “Tell me more” and “What happened next?”
- Separate what participants did from what they say they might do.
- Record negative cases and disconfirming evidence.
- Avoid praising the idea or explaining intended benefits.
- Capture direct quotes only with consent and anonymize them in reporting.
