# Prototype Usability Test Protocol

## Objective

Assess whether participants can discover, configure, understand, cancel, and recover from the proposed After Hours experience without moderator assistance.

## Format

- Two moderated rounds
- 5-8 participants per round
- 35-45 minutes per session
- Existing interactive prototype used as a concept simulation
- Think-aloud encouraged, but task completion recorded separately from verbal explanation

## Critical tasks

### Task 1 - Discover the control

Scenario: You are watching late at night and expect to fall asleep within about 30 minutes. Show what you would do.

Measure:

- Finds the control without help
- Time to first relevant action
- Incorrect controls selected
- Interpretation of the moon icon

### Task 2 - Configure an end condition

Scenario: Set the experience to stop after 30 minutes. Then show how you would choose to finish the current episode instead.

Measure:

- Completion success
- Comprehension of duration versus end-of-episode
- Confidence rating

### Task 3 - Interpret dimming and audio behaviour

Scenario: The feature has started transitioning. Explain what you believe is happening and what will happen next.

Measure:

- Understands countdown, dimming, audio state, and final stop
- Identifies whether playback continues or pauses
- Notes discomfort, accessibility, or control concerns

### Task 4 - Cancel an unwanted transition

Scenario: Another person is still awake and wants to continue watching. Show how they would prevent playback from stopping.

Measure:

- Cancellation success
- Time to cancel
- Recovery of normal brightness and audio
- Perceived control

### Task 5 - Recover the viewing position

Scenario: It is the next day. Find the point where you were last actively watching and resume.

Measure:

- Finds recovery card
- Understands why the timestamp was saved
- Chooses resume versus restart appropriately
- Confidence in bookmark accuracy

### Task 6 - Interpret wearable permission

Scenario: You are offered automatic sleep detection through a wearable. Explain what data you believe would be used and decide whether to enable it.

Measure:

- Permission comprehension
- Expected data flow
- Comfort rating
- Misconceptions and trust conditions

## Success definitions

| Result | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Success | Completed without moderator help |
| Assisted success | Completed after one neutral prompt |
| Failure | Could not complete or formed a materially unsafe misunderstanding |

Round 2 passes the proposed pilot usability threshold when at least 80% complete every critical pilot task without intervention and no more than 10% misunderstand cancellation or believe the system can stop playback against their intent.

## Issue severity

| Severity | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Prevents task completion or creates loss of control, privacy, or accessibility risk |
| High | Causes repeated failure or a materially wrong mental model |
| Medium | Causes delay, uncertainty, or recoverable error |
| Low | Cosmetic, wording, or preference issue with little task impact |

## Post-task questions

- How confident are you that you completed the task? 1-5
- What did you expect to happen?
- What, if anything, surprised you?
- How would you improve this step?

## Session close

- Single Ease Question for each task
- Overall perceived usefulness
- Most valuable and least valuable capability
- Likelihood of monthly use
- Open concerns

## Known prototype limitations

- The prototype does not process real video, audio, health data, or cloud bookmarks.
- Simulated timing does not prove technical feasibility.
- External image and icon dependencies may affect rendering.
- Participants must be told that the experience is an independent concept.
