# Phase 2 Secondary-Evidence Validation Charter

**Status:** Approved direction; research synthesis complete  
**Date:** 2026-11-08  
**Project:** Netflix After Hours — independent portfolio product case

## Decision context

The original Phase 1 plan included direct survey, interview, and usability recruitment. Because this is an independent portfolio case and not work commissioned by Netflix, no public research will be presented as Netflix-sponsored, and no respondent recruitment will use Netflix branding or imply access to Netflix customers.

Phase 2 therefore uses public secondary evidence to validate, weaken, replace, or retire the concept baselines. This is a research-method decision, not legal advice.

## Research objective

Determine whether the public evidence is strong enough to justify:

1. A credible customer problem;
2. A differentiated first viable release;
3. A technically plausible product direction;
4. A transparent, scenario-based economic thesis; and
5. A defensible portfolio narrative without presenting projections as measured Netflix results.

## Evidence questions

1. How common is viewing near bedtime?
2. Is falling asleep during video playback established in the relevant adult streaming population?
3. Do current platforms already offer timers, reminders, or autoplay controls?
4. What remains differentiated after those existing alternatives are considered?
5. Are dimming, advanced audio, and wearable-trigger claims supported by science and platform capabilities?
6. Can public evidence quantify unattended playback, recovery success, retention, Premium conversion, or Netflix-specific cost savings?
7. Which assumptions can be retained only as future experiment targets?

## Evidence hierarchy

| Tier | Evidence type | Use in this case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, large peer-reviewed studies, official platform documentation | May support or challenge a directional product conclusion |
| 2 | Smaller peer-reviewed studies and reputable measurement panels | May support plausibility with population and method caveats |
| 3 | Official product support pages and company documentation | May establish feature availability, platform constraints, or architecture facts |
| 4 | Reputable industry surveys and trade publications | May establish precedent or context, but not Netflix outcomes |
| 5 | Commentary, unsourced claims, prototype copy, and internal concept projections | Cannot validate a baseline |

## Verdict definitions

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Multiple relevant sources support the claim's direction and the claim is appropriately bounded |
| Partially supported | The direction is plausible, but the population, measure, recency, or causal interpretation is limited |
| Unresolved | Public evidence is insufficient to decide |
| Contradicted or overstated | Evidence conflicts with the claim or the language exceeds what the evidence supports |
| Retired as a baseline | The number or assertion must not be used as an observed fact |

## Boundary of validation

Secondary evidence can validate the **existence and plausibility** of a problem and identify market patterns. It cannot establish:

- Netflix customer adoption;
- causal retention impact;
- Premium conversion;
- actual unattended-playback minutes;
- Netflix-specific marginal delivery cost;
- prototype usability;
- production performance; or
- UAT or deployment readiness.

Those outcomes require first-party telemetry, an authorized experiment, primary research, or internal cost data.

## Independence and public-use guardrails

- Every public artifact must state that the work is independent and unaffiliated with Netflix.
- Do not use language such as “Netflix study,” “Netflix users told us,” or “our customers.”
- Cite sources and preserve their stated population, method, geography, and date.
- Do not convert association into causation.
- Do not describe health or wellness features as treating, improving, or protecting sleep without suitable clinical evidence and review.
- Label all economic inputs as public facts, research estimates, product targets, or scenario assumptions.
- If primary research is later conducted, use neutral streaming-language, independent consent text, adult participants, no collection of Netflix credentials or account data, and no implication of Netflix sponsorship.

## Phase 2 deliverables

1. Deep research findings;
2. Baseline validation matrix;
3. Revised baselines and product recommendations;
4. An updated hypothesis register and release decision basis.

## Completion standard

Phase 2 is complete when every material concept claim has a verdict, a source basis, an explicit limitation, and a product consequence.
